How to

Linear motion, made easy 🚀

Everything in Dueply uses one toolkit: motion in a straight line with constant acceleration. Learn these 4 equations and a few word clues and you can solve almost every question.

🎯 What is this topic, and why does it matter?

Constant acceleration means the speed changes by the same amount every second (a car gaining 2 m/s every second, or gravity pulling things down). That is why one set of equations works.

Projectiles? Anything dropped or thrown straight up or down is this topic, with gravity as the acceleration. Angled projectiles (like a football kicked at an angle) come later, but they reuse the same equations: sideways has no acceleration, up and down has gravity.

Why it’s the base: it is the foundation of Leaving Cert Applied Maths (and physics). It also links to calculus in Higher Level Maths: speed is the rate of change of distance, and acceleration is the rate of change of speed.

🔤 What each letter means

sDisplacement: how far you are from where you started, in a direction (metres, m). Not always the same as distance travelled!

uInitial speed: how fast it’s going at the start of the bit you’re looking at (m/s). “u” comes before “v”.

vFinal speed: how fast it’s going at the end of that bit (m/s).

aAcceleration: how much the speed changes every second (m/s²). Slowing down is a negative a, also called retardation or deceleration.

tTime taken (seconds, s).

💡 Each equation has four of these five letters. Every question gives you three, and you hunt for the one you’re missing.
🧰 The 4 equations (and the missing-letter trick)

Pick the equation by the letter you don’t have and don’t need:

v = u + atno s needed
s = ut + ½at²no v needed
v² = u² + 2asno t needed
s = ½(u + v)tno a needed

Why they work: v = u + at says “new speed = old speed + the speed gained”. The distance one uses the average speed, (u+v)/2, times the time. The others come from swapping one into the other.

🗺️ A 4-step method that always works

1. Draw a quick sketch and pick a positive direction (stick with it!).

2. Write the list s, u, v, a, t and fill in what the question tells you (use the word clues below).

3. Find the equation with the letters you have, plus the one you want.

4. Solve, then ask: does the answer make sense? Include the unit.

🕵️ Word decoder: what the wording secretly tells you
“dropped”, “released”, “starts from rest”, “falls from rest”
u = 0 (it starts still). But v is NOT 0: it speeds up as it falls, so it’s going fast when it lands. Why? You let go, you didn’t throw it.
“comes to rest”, “stops”, “brought to rest”
v = 0 at the end. Why? Rest means speed zero.
“thrown / projected upwards”
u is the throw speed. At the very top the ball stops for an instant, so v = 0 at the maximum height (not at the start, and not at the end!). Why? It has to stop before it can turn around.
“thrown downwards”
u is the throw speed (not 0), and gravity speeds it up. Why? You gave it a push, and gravity adds more.
“uniform / constant speed”
a = 0. Why? Speed isn’t changing. Then simply distance = speed × time.
“retardation”, “decelerates”, “brakes”
a is negative if forwards is positive. Why? It’s slowing down.
“falls”, “under gravity”
Use g = 9.8 m/s². Down is positive if it’s falling; for things thrown up, a = −9.8. Why? Gravity always pulls down, even while the ball is going up (that’s why it slows).
“returns to the starting point”
s = 0 (back where it began).
“in the 7th second”
The distance during that one second only: s₇ − s₆ (distance in 7 s minus distance in 6 s). Why? “In the 7th second” means between 6 and 7 seconds.
“meet”, “collide”, “passes”
Same place, same time: set the two s values equal (mind who started later).
trains/cars “pass each other”
Opposite directions: add the lengths, and the speeds add too (relative speed). Same direction: subtract the speeds.
“average speed”
Total distance ÷ total time. The shortcut (u+v)/2 only works for one stretch of constant acceleration.
🍎 Gravity, up and down (projectiles)

Going up, the ball slows by 9.8 m/s every second. Coming down, it speeds up by 9.8 m/s every second. So:

⬆️ Time up = time down (it’s symmetrical).
⏸️ At the top: v = 0.
🔁 It lands back at the start with the same speed it left with, just pointing down.
⏱️ Time to the top: t = u ÷ 9.8. Max height: h = u² ÷ 19.6.

⚠️ “Between two instants it is 39.2 m high” (a ball going up and coming down): you get two times, once on the way up, once on the way down. The answer is the difference between them.
⚠️ Traps that catch people
Distance ≠ displacement. A ball thrown up and back down travels 2× the height as distance, but its displacement is smaller. Read whether it asks for “distance travelled” or “how far from the start”.
Units. Check minutes → seconds (2 minutes = 120 s) and km/h → m/s (divide by 3.6).
Quadratics give two answers. A negative time (or one that doesn’t fit the story) is thrown away.
Signs. Pick up or down as positive and stay consistent; mixing them is the number one cause of wrong answers.
Don’t assume v = 0. Only use it for “comes to rest”, “stops” or “at the top”.
Two stages? The final speed of the first stage is the initial speed (u) of the next.
📈 Speed–time graphs

Speed on the up axis, time along the bottom.

📐 Gradient (slope) = acceleration. Steeper means a bigger a. Flat means a = 0 (constant speed). Sloping down means slowing.

🟦 Area under the graph = distance. A triangle is ½ × base × height, and a trapezium is the average of the parallel sides × width.

Questions like “accelerates, cruises, then brakes” are a trapezium: ramp up, flat, ramp down.

🧪 Two worked examples

Thrown up: A ball is thrown up at 39.2 m/s. How long to reach the top?
u = 39.2, v = 0 (top!), a = −9.8, t = ? → v = u + at → 0 = 39.2 − 9.8t → t = 4 s ✅

Dropped: A ball is dropped from 78.4 m. How long to land?
s = 78.4, u = 0 (dropped), a = 9.8, t = ? → s = ut + ½at² → 78.4 = 4.9t² → t = 4 s ✅
Its landing speed: v = 0 + 9.8 × 4 = 39.2 m/s, so v was definitely not 0!

Tip: use g = 9.8 m/s² in every Dueply question.
